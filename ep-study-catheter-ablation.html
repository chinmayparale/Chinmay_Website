EP Study & Catheter Ablation in Solapur & Pune

What is an EP study?

Thin wires (catheters) are passed through veins in the groin into the heart. They record the heart's electrical signals from inside and can gently trigger the rhythm problem in a controlled setting, so that its mechanism and location can be identified precisely.

What is catheter ablation?

Once the source is found, a catheter delivers controlled energy — usually radiofrequency — to a very small area of tissue that is causing or sustaining the abnormal rhythm. 3D electroanatomic mapping builds a detailed model of the heart chamber, allowing precise treatment with less X-ray exposure. Rhythm problems treated

  • Supraventricular tachycardia (SVT) — including AVNRT and accessory-pathway tachycardias such as WPW syndrome. Ablation is often curative.
  • Atrial flutter
  • Atrial fibrillation (AF) — treated with pulmonary vein isolation (PVI)
  • Ventricular ectopics (PVCs) — especially when frequent, symptomatic or weakening the heart
  • Ventricular tachycardia (VT) — including scar-related VT after a heart attack

Who should consider ablation?

  • Recurrent palpitations from SVT, particularly if you prefer not to take lifelong medicines
  • Symptomatic atrial fibrillation despite medicines, or as an early treatment in selected patients
  • Frequent PVCs causing symptoms or reduced heart function
  • Ventricular tachycardia, including patients receiving repeated ICD shocks

How the procedure is done

Most ablations are done under local anaesthesia with sedation; longer procedures such as AF or VT ablation may use deeper sedation or general anaesthesia. Depending on the rhythm, the procedure takes one to four hours. Many patients go home the next day.

Recovery

Most people return to light activity within a few days and avoid heavy lifting for about a week. After AF ablation, blood thinners and rhythm medicines are usually continued for some time as advised.

Frequently asked questions

Is catheter ablation a permanent cure?

For many SVTs, including WPW syndrome, ablation is usually a permanent cure. For atrial fibrillation and some ventricular arrhythmias, a single procedure controls the rhythm in most patients, but some need a repeat procedure.

Is an EP study or ablation painful?

The procedure is done through veins in the groin under local anaesthesia with sedation, so most patients feel little discomfort. Longer procedures may use deeper sedation or general anaesthesia.

How soon can I return to work after ablation?

Many patients return to desk work within a few days. Heavy lifting and strenuous exercise are usually avoided for about a week.

What is the difference between an EP study and an ablation?

An EP study is the diagnostic part that identifies the type and location of the rhythm problem. Ablation is the treatment, and is usually performed in the same sitting once the source is found.
