What is mitral regurgitation?

The mitral valve separates the left upper and lower chambers of the heart. When it does not close properly, blood leaks backwards with every heartbeat — this is mitral regurgitation.

  • Primary (degenerative) — the valve itself is damaged, for example a prolapsing or torn (flail) leaflet
  • Secondary (functional) — the valve is normal but the heart muscle has weakened and enlarged, pulling the leaflets apart, as seen in heart failure

Symptoms include breathlessness, tiredness, swelling of the feet and palpitations, often from atrial fibrillation.

What is MitraClip?

MitraClip is a form of transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (TEER). A catheter is passed from a vein in the groin to the right side of the heart, then across the thin wall between the upper chambers (transseptal puncture) into the left atrium. A small clip grasps the two mitral leaflets at the point where they leak and holds them together, reducing the leak. More than one clip may be used.

Who is suitable?

  • Severe, symptomatic primary mitral regurgitation in patients at high risk for open-heart surgery
  • Severe secondary mitral regurgitation that persists despite optimal heart failure medicines and, where indicated, cardiac resynchronisation therapy

Suitability depends heavily on valve anatomy, which is studied in detail with a transoesophageal echocardiogram (TOE). The decision is made with the Heart Team.

How the procedure is done

The procedure is done in the cardiac catheterisation lab, usually under general anaesthesia so that a TOE probe can guide every step in real time. It typically takes two to three hours. Nothing is cut on the chest; only a puncture in the groin vein is needed.

Recovery

Most patients walk the next day and go home after a short stay. Heart failure medicines continue, and a follow-up echocardiogram checks the result. Many patients notice less breathlessness and fewer hospital admissions for heart failure.

Frequently asked questions

Is MitraClip a surgery?

No. MitraClip is a catheter procedure done through a vein in the groin. The chest is not opened and a heart-lung machine is not used.

Will MitraClip completely stop the leak?

MitraClip usually reduces the leak substantially but may not remove it completely. In suitable patients this improves breathlessness and quality of life and can reduce hospital admissions for heart failure.

Can MitraClip be done if I already have a pacemaker or CRT device?

Yes. Having a pacemaker, ICD or CRT device does not by itself prevent MitraClip. Suitability depends mainly on the anatomy of the mitral valve.
