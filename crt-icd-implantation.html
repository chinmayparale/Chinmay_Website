Implantable cardioverter-defibrillator (ICD)

An ICD continuously monitors the heart rhythm. If it detects a dangerous fast rhythm — ventricular tachycardia (VT) or ventricular fibrillation (VF) — it treats it within seconds with rapid pacing or a shock.

An ICD is usually advised for people who:

  • Have survived a cardiac arrest or a sustained ventricular arrhythmia
  • Have a significantly weakened heart (ejection fraction around 35% or less) despite optimal medicines for at least three months
  • Have certain inherited heart conditions with a high risk of sudden death

Cardiac resynchronisation therapy (CRT)

In many patients with heart failure, an electrical delay called left bundle branch block (LBBB) makes the two walls of the main pumping chamber contract out of step, wasting energy. CRT paces the heart so that it contracts in a coordinated way again.

CRT is usually considered for patients with a weak heart (ejection fraction around 35% or less), a wide QRS on ECG — especially LBBB — and symptoms despite medicines. In suitable patients CRT improves symptoms, heart function and survival.

  • CRT-P — resynchronisation pacemaker
  • CRT-D — resynchronisation with a built-in defibrillator

Newer approaches: left bundle CRT and LOT-CRT

Conventional CRT uses a lead in a vein on the outside of the heart (coronary sinus). Newer techniques use conduction system pacing to resynchronise the heart through its own wiring:

  • Left bundle branch area pacing CRT (LBBAP-CRT)
  • Left bundle optimised CRT (LOT-CRT) — combining left bundle pacing with a coronary sinus lead

Dr. Parale has published comparisons of these two strategies in Pacing and Clinical Electrophysiology (PACE) and the Indian Pacing and Electrophysiology Journal.

How the procedure is done

Under local anaesthesia with sedation, leads are passed through a vein below the collarbone into the heart and connected to the device placed under the skin. An ICD implant typically takes about an hour; CRT takes longer because of the additional left-sided lead. Most patients go home within one to two days.

Living with a CRT or ICD

  • Continue your heart failure medicines — the device works alongside them, not instead of them
  • Regular device check-ups, and remote monitoring where available
  • If an ICD delivers a shock, contact your doctor; seek emergency help if you receive more than one shock or feel unwell
  • Your doctor will advise on driving and return to work

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between CRT-P and CRT-D?

CRT-P is a resynchronisation pacemaker that helps the heart pump in a coordinated way. CRT-D does the same and also contains a defibrillator that can treat life-threatening fast heart rhythms.

Does an ICD shock hurt?

A shock is felt as a sudden strong jolt in the chest that lasts a moment. Many dangerous rhythms are treated with painless rapid pacing before a shock is needed.

Will CRT cure heart failure?

CRT does not cure heart failure, but in suitable patients it improves symptoms, heart function and survival. Heart failure medicines must be continued alongside the device.

What is LOT-CRT?

Left bundle optimised CRT (LOT-CRT) combines left bundle branch area pacing with a coronary sinus lead to achieve more complete resynchronisation in selected heart failure patients.
